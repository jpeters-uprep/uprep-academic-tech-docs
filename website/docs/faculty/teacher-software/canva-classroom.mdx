This guide covers setup for those new to Canva Classroom, including those moving
from Padlet or Trello.

**Canva Classroom isn't a separate product** — it's the classroom-management
area inside Canva Education, reached from the **Classroom** item in the left nav
once you're logged in. Everything below happens inside that same Canva Education
account.

## Overview

Canva for Education is a secure-by-design tool that combines the collaborative
"wall" features of Padlet with the organizational power of Trello—all within a
private classroom ecosystem.

## How to Access Canva for Education

1. Go to https://www.canva.com/
2. Log in using your UPrep account.

## Setting Up Your Class

1. Click the Classroom on the left column.
2. Under "Classes", create a class (e.g., "9th Grade Computer Science"). You can
   also scroll down to find your existing class if you already made one.
3. Click the invite icon in the top right of your class page to open **Invite
   people to your class**. Click **Copy invite link** to share a link, or use
   **Invite via email** to add specific students directly. You can only invite
   people with a UPrep email address.

This creates a "walled garden" where only your invited students can see and
interact with class materials.

## Checking Student Work

The **Classroom** home screen (the same place you went to create your class)
also shows your assignments dashboard:

- **Assignments → View all / To review / Reviewed** lists everything you've
  assigned, split by review status.
- **Submissions** shows a running count of student work waiting for you to
  review, with a shortcut straight to it.
- **Recent Assignments**, on the same screen, shows your most recently created
  or active assignments, with a **+ Create** shortcut to start a new one.

This is the fastest way to see what's outstanding without opening each
assignment individually.

Inside a specific class, the same information is broken out under two tabs:
**Assignments** (each assignment's name, who it's assigned to, due date, and
completion rate) and **People** (your class roster).

## Creating Whiteboards (The Padlet/Trello Alternative)

If you are used to Padlet’s "walls" or Trello’s "boards," you will want to use
Canva Whiteboards.

1. On the homepage, click the "Create" icon.
2. To mimic Padlet: Search templates for "Whiteboard" or "Brainstorm Whiteboard"
3. Design your whiteboard from the template using prompts, spaces for student
   sticky notes, or whatever other design works for you.
4. Sticky Note Hack: Once on a board, simply press the S key on your keyboard to
   instantly create a sticky note.

## Collaboration: Reacting and Commenting

To make the board interactive while keeping students accountable:

- Reacting (Like/Emoji): Click on any element or sticky note. Select the Emoji
  icon from the floating toolbar. You can see a count of how many students
  "liked" or "voted" for an idea.

- Commenting: Right-click an element and select "Comment." This opens a thread
  where students can give feedback.

- Accountability: Unlike Padlet, student names and avatars are attached to every
  reaction and comment. There is no anonymous posting, ensuring a safer digital
  environment.

## Sharing with the Class

1. In the top right of your design, click "Assign."
2. In the **Assign to students** panel, search for and select your class (or
   click **Create a class** to make one on the spot).
3. Students can now jump onto the board and start collaborating in real-time.
