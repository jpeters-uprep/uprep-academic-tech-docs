Our printers are Bambu Lab machines — a fleet of X1 Carbons plus an H2C. You
prepare a file in **Bambu Studio** (the slicer), which cuts your model into
layers and generates instructions the printer follows.

For getting a print made, the two routes — join the queue, or slice and print it
yourself in the Makerspace — are documented on
[Makers Tools](/makers/makers-tools#3d-printers) and the
[3D Print Queue](/makers/print-queue) page. This page is about the part that's
yours: making a model that prints well and slicing it sensibly.

Bambu Lab's own documentation is thorough and current — start with their
[Introduction to Bambu Studio](https://wiki.bambulab.com/en/x1/manual/introduction-to-bambu-studio)
and
[Print Settings reference](https://wiki.bambulab.com/en/bambu-studio/parameter).

## Design decisions that happen before you slice

**Orientation is a strength decision.** A printed part is layers glued together,
and it's much weaker across the layer lines than along them. A hook printed flat
on the plate will snap where a hook printed upright would hold. Ask which
direction the load comes from, then orient so the layers resist it.

**Overhangs need support past about 45 degrees.** Plastic printed out over thin
air droops. Under roughly 45 degrees from vertical, the layer below holds it up
fine. Steeper than that and you either add supports or redesign — a chamfer or
fillet often removes the need for supports entirely, and prints cleaner than
supports ever will.

**Design for the nozzle.** Nothing thinner than about two nozzle widths will
print as a solid wall. Very fine detail, sharp knife edges, and text under a few
millimeters tall come out mushy.

**Big and hollow beats small and solid.** Print time scales with volume. A
thicker wall and modest infill is stronger, faster, and cheaper than a solid
part.

See [Tolerance and Fit](../tolerance-and-fit) for the clearance to leave between
a printed part and whatever it has to mate with.

## Slicing in Bambu Studio

1. **Import your file.** Export 3MF from Fusion rather than STL — see
   [Exporting Your Design](../exporting-your-design) for why.
2. **Select the right printer and nozzle.** Our machines are not all identical;
   picking the wrong profile is a wasted plate.
3. **Select the filament actually loaded.** PLA and PETG print at very different
   temperatures. Bambu's
   [filament compatibility table](https://wiki.bambulab.com/en/general/filament-guide-material-table)
   covers what each material needs.
4. **Orient and place the part** on the plate, per the decisions above.
5. **Set layer height.** Thinner layers look better and take longer. For a
   functional bracket, a standard layer height is fine; save fine layers for
   parts someone will look at closely.
6. **Add supports only where you need them.** Bambu's
   [support settings](https://wiki.bambulab.com/en/software/bambu-studio/support)
   and
   [support painting](https://wiki.bambulab.com/en/software/bambu-studio/support-painting)
   guides show how to place supports on specific overhangs instead of blanketing
   the whole model.
7. **Slice, then actually look at the preview.** Step through the layers. Look
   for parts floating with nothing beneath them, walls that came out
   single-thickness, and a first layer that's smaller than you expected.
8. **Check the time and material estimate.** If a part is going to run eight
   hours, that's a scheduling fact your team needs to know today, not tomorrow.

## Print time is a shared resource

Five printers, eighteen students, and a semester with real deadlines:

- **Sequence your prints.** Print the part you need for a test fit first, not
  the decorative one.
- **Fail small.** Print a small test section of a big part to check fit before
  committing to the full thing.
- **A reprint costs the same time twice.** Ten minutes checking the preview is
  the cheapest insurance available.

## When a print fails

Bambu Lab documents the common failure modes with photographs, which is far more
useful than a text description:

- [Common print quality problems and solutions](https://wiki.bambulab.com/en/knowledge-sharing/common-print-quality-problem)
- [Bad print quality: causes and solutions](https://wiki.bambulab.com/en/knowledge-sharing/troubleshooting-printing-issues)
- [Filament not extruding](https://wiki.bambulab.com/en/x1/troubleshooting/filament-not-extruding)

Identify the failure before you change anything. A part that came loose from the
plate, a part that warped, and a part that printed at the wrong scale look
different and have different fixes — and one of those three is a units problem
in your export, not a printer problem at all.

Log the failure in your journal with a photo. A photographed failure plus what
you changed next is the clearest evidence of your process you can produce.
