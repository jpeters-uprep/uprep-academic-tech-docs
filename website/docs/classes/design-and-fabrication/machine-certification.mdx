You get certified on a machine before you run it on your own. Certification
isn't a formality or a quiz — it's the point at which you can work independently
instead of waiting for an adult to be free every time you need a cut.

Everyone is new to some of these machines. Nobody is expected to arrive already
knowing them.

## How it works

1. **Watch the demo** for that machine and read this site's page for it.
2. **Run it once with June alongside you** — a small, real job, not a
   hypothetical one.
3. **Show the steps yourself**, narrating what you're doing and why. That's the
   certification.

Ask for a certification slot when you're ready rather than waiting to be
scheduled. The CNC in particular has one machine and a whole class behind it, so
early is better.

## Before anything: general shop expectations

These apply everywhere and aren't machine-specific:

- Correct [PPE](/makers/ppe) for the task, on before you start.
- Closed-toe shoes.
- Nothing loose — hair tied back, sleeves up, no dangling jewelry or lanyards.
- **No gloves near anything that spins.** The exception-free rule.
- You clean up your station and the machine before you leave it.
- You tell an adult when something breaks, jams, or sounds wrong. Reporting a
  problem is never the thing that gets you in trouble.

## CNC router (Shapeoko)

The longest certification, because it has the most ways to go wrong. Full
workflow on [Running the CNC](../cnc-workflow).

You'll show that you can:

- Name the PPE this machine needs and why hearing protection applies to
  bystanders too.
- Secure stock so it cannot shift, and explain where the clamps are relative to
  your toolpath.
- Install a bit and tighten the collet correctly.
- Initialize the machine and set X, Y, and Z zero — and say what happens if Z
  zero is wrong.
- Load a job, check the preview, and run an air pass.
- Start a cut, stay with it, and stop it. Show me the stop.
- List the sounds and signs that mean stop immediately.
- Shut down, retrieve parts, and vacuum the machine and floor.

## 3D printers (Bambu Lab)

Workflow on [3D Printing Workflow](../3d-printing-workflow).

You'll show that you can:

- Slice a file in Bambu Studio with the correct printer and filament profile
  selected.
- Read the sliced preview and identify supports, the first layer, and the
  estimated time.
- Send a job and confirm it started correctly.
- Remove a finished print from the plate without damaging the plate.
- Recognize a failed print early, stop it, and describe what went wrong.
- Explain why you don't reach into a printer mid-print — the nozzle and bed are
  hot and the gantry moves.

## Vinyl and cardstock cutter (Silhouette Cameo)

Workflow on [Vinyl and Cardstock Cutting](../vinyl-and-cardstock-cutter).

You'll show that you can:

- Load a mat and material squarely.
- Choose the correct blade and material setting, and run a test cut first.
- Handle and change a blade safely.
- Explain why vinyl never goes in the laser cutter.
- Weed a cut and apply HTV or adhesive vinyl using the correct method for each.

## Hand tools, drills, and the drill press

Details on [Hand Tools and Joinery](../hand-tools-and-joinery).

You'll show that you can:

- Clamp work before drilling it.
- Select the right bit for a pass-through hole versus a hole a screw bites into.
- Drill without blowing out the exit face.
- Use calipers to take a measurement you can act on.
- Explain the bare-hands rule around the drill press.

## Staying certified

Certification is about being trusted with a machine, so it rests on how you
behave around it, not just on one demonstration. Skipping PPE, walking away from
a running job, or working around a safety step after being reminded puts it at
risk. A single honest mistake, corrected once you're reminded, doesn't — that
happens to everyone who learns a machine.

If you haven't touched a machine in a while and feel rusty, ask for a refresher
run. That's a normal request, not an admission of anything.
