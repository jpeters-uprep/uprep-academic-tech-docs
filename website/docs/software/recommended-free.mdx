# Recommended Free Software

## Desmos

Website: [Desmos](https://www.desmos.com)

Desmos is a free, full-featured graphing calculator. Widely used for algebra,
functions, geometry, and inquiry-based math lessons.

## Desmos Classroom (via Amplify)

Website: [Amplify Classroom](https://classroom.amplify.com)

Desmos's classroom activities, Activity Builder, and Computation Layer moved to
Amplify — teachers now go to `classroom.amplify.com`, not `teacher.desmos.com`.
Still free.

## GeoGebra

Website: [GeoGebra](https://www.geogebra.org)

GeoGebra is a set of free geometry, graphing, algebra, and statistics tools.
Great for constructing diagrams, exploring functions, and modeling.

## Google Arts & Culture

Website: [Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com)

A free, rich digital platform that brings artworks, historical sites, and
cultural stories from around the world into the classroom with immersive tours,
360° views, and ready-to-use teacher resources.

## Google Scholar

Website: [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com)

A free search engine for scholarly articles, academic papers, and citations.
Useful for high school research, extended essays, and data gathering.

## Kahoot

Website: [Kahoot](https://kahoot.com)

Free tier - a gamified quiz platform for fast-paced formative assessment. Great
for engagement and quick knowledge checks. The free Kahoot! Go plan for K-12
teachers supports up to 40 participants in both Present and Assign modes.

## Minecraft Education

Website: [Minecraft Education](https://education.minecraft.net)

Minecraft Education prepares students
for the future workplace by helping build skills like collaboration, creative
problem-solving, communication, and systems thinking. Available for Windows,
ChromeOS, Mac, iPad, and Android.

## NatGeo Edu

Website:
[National Geographic Education](https://www.nationalgeographic.org/education)

National Geographic for Education is a rich collection of maps, videos, lesson
plans, and interactive resources that bring geography, science, and exploration
to life. Great for supporting inquiry-based learning and global awareness across
all subjects.

## PhET Simulations

Website: [PhET Interactive Simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu)

PhET has free interactive science simulations covering physics, chemistry, and
biology. Ideal for conceptual understanding and lab-replacement activities.

## Wayground (formerly Quizizz)

Website: [Wayground](https://www.wayground.com)

Wayground (formerly Quizizz) free tier - a modern quiz tool with homework mode,
analytics, and a massive question bank. Free tier limits: 100 participants per
session, limited storage and question types, and no LMS integration.

## Science Friday

Website: [Science Friday](https://www.sciencefriday.com)

Science Friday provides high-quality STEM journalism, videos, and teacher
resources. Excellent for phenomenon-based instruction and real-world science
connections.

## Scratch

Website: [Scratch](https://scratch.mit.edu)

Scratch (from MIT) is a great environment for learning coding with block
programming; it is free-form and focused on creating music, games, and
animation.

## CodeAI (formerly Code.org)

Website: [CodeAI](https://code.org)

Code.org rebranded to CodeAI in June 2026, pivoting toward AI literacy alongside
its original programming lessons — `code.org` still resolves. New: a free "AI
Foundations" high school course, and an Hour of AI alongside the existing Hour
of Code.
