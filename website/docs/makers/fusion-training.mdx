Autodesk publishes a full
[self-paced training collection for Fusion](https://www.autodesk.com/learn/ondemand/collection/self-paced-learning-for-fusion)
covering everything from the basics to advanced workflows. That collection is
huge, so this page picks out the lessons most relevant to what we actually build
at UPrep. See the [Autodesk software page](/software/fully-supported/autodesk)
for how to get access to Fusion itself. Design and Fabrication students should
also see
[Fusion in This Class](/classes/design-and-fabrication/fusion-in-this-class) for
files, units, and handoff.

## Assemblies and Joints

Once a project is more than one part, you need components that move (or stay
put) relative to each other. Fusion's Joint tools define that motion. Autodesk's
reference documentation is the place to start:

- [As-built joints](https://help.autodesk.com/view/NINVFUS/ENU/?guid=ASM-AS-BUILT-JOINT)
  — for components already positioned where you want them, which is the usual
  case when you've modeled everything in place.
- [Adding Joints and As-built Joints to components](https://help.autodesk.com/view/NINVFUS/ENU/?guid=GUID-DE65A081-8233-456E-A930-F0D5FEB94277)
  — a full guided activity assembling a camera stand, covering rigid, revolute,
  slider, and ball joints.
- [Duplicate components with their joints](https://help.autodesk.com/view/NINVFUS/ENU/?guid=ASM-DUPLICATE-WITH-JOINTS)
  — for repeated hardware, so you place a joint once instead of twenty times.

## Exporting for the CNC, printers, and cutters

Getting a design out of Fusion in the format a machine wants:

- [Export sketch as DXF](https://help.autodesk.com/view/NINVFUS/ENU/?guid=GUID-BC1A5C26-F7DC-499B-8411-5C191784BA3D)
  — for the CNC router and the vinyl/cardstock cutters.
- [How to export a 3D model to DXF](https://www.autodesk.com/support/technical/article/caas/sfdcarticles/sfdcarticles/How-to-export-a-3D-model-to-dxf-for-cnc-routing-plasma-cutting-or-waterjet-cutting-from-Fusion-360.html)
  — when what you want to cut is the outline of a solid body rather than an
  existing sketch.
- [Mesh export](https://help.autodesk.com/view/NINVFUS/ENU/?guid=MESH-EXPORT-TOOLS)
  and
  [3D print a design](https://help.autodesk.com/view/NINVFUS/ENU/?guid=SLD-3D-PRINT)
  — for 3MF and STL files headed to Bambu Studio.

For which format goes to which machine, and the export mistakes that cost
machine time, see
[Exporting Your Design](/classes/design-and-fabrication/exporting-your-design).

## Designing to Cut and Score Cardboard (2D → 3D)

Sheet metal designs in Fusion are useful for anything you bend or score and then
fold from flat into a 3D shape. The following are part of Autodesk's
[Sheet Metal with Fusion](https://www.autodesk.com/learn/ondemand/curated/sheet-metal-with-fusion-360)
curated series. Designs can be cut by hand, with the Silhouette Cameos, or the
laser cutter by exporting DXF files into the appropriate software.

- [Creating a sheet metal component](https://www.autodesk.com/learn/ondemand/curated/sheet-metal-with-fusion-360/m5DWvSQ3qciY8UUgG0j3d)
- [Using rules in sheet metal](https://www.autodesk.com/learn/ondemand/curated/sheet-metal-with-fusion-360/7blVVgTEDSgzzUQTHNHWMd)
  - You'll need to set up cardboard as a material type yourself, and this video
    covers how to do that.
